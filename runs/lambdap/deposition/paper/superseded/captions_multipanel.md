# Figure captions (draft)

**Fig. 1 | Deposition footprint scales with local plan area density, deposited mass does not.**
Per-source deposition metrics at t = 300 s of the transient run, against the plan area density
λp evaluated in a 75 m radius window (the radius that maximises the rank correlation, Fig. 2a).
**a**, Footprint area A90, the ground area holding 90 % of the deposited particles (10 m cells).
**b**, Fraction of deposited particles within 150 m of the source. **c**, Fraction of released
particles deposited. Filled symbols, street-level sources; open symbols, the three sources inside
enclosed courtyards (S3, S6, S10; §5.3 of the morphology analysis). Lines are least-squares fits to
the ten street-level sources; ρs is the Spearman rank correlation over the same ten sources.
Numbers label the sources.

**Fig. 2 | The form scale that deposition responds to, and the approach to steady deposition.**
**a**, Spearman rank correlation between each deposition metric and λp as a function of the window
radius R, for the ten street-level sources. Shading marks R = 75–100 m, where the three shape
metrics peak. **b**, Deposited fraction against time for each source (release starts at t = 150 s);
red, enclosed courtyard sources. No source has reached steady state by t = 300 s.

**Fig. 3 | Deposition maps, transient versus frozen mean flow.**
Deposited particles per 100 m² cell at t = 300 s. **a**, Transient LES. **b**, Lagrangian model
driven by the frozen 150-s mean field. **c**, Difference of the logarithms; red, more deposition in
the transient case; blue, more in the mean flow. Circles mark the 13 sources. Both runs use the
same release schedule and particle count (1,299,844 released).

**Fig. 4 | The frozen mean flow reproduces where material deposits but not how far it spreads.**
Per-source comparison of **a**, deposited fraction and **b**, footprint area A90 between the two
runs; the line is 1:1. Symbols as in Fig. 1. The mean-flow run deposits 8 % more material overall
while confining it to 80 % of the footprint area (median ratios).

**Fig. 7 | Urban form sets where pedestrian exposure concentrates, not how much of it there is.**
**a**, Time-integrated exposure below 2 m for four sources spanning the density range (log scale, shared
colour limits; circle marks the source). **b–d**, Exposure area (cells above 1 % of the peak), peak
exposure and exposure per released particle against λp. Filled symbols, street-level sources; open
symbols, enclosed courtyards. **e**, Rank correlation with λp for six height bands: the spatial metrics
are insensitive to the band, the total is not.

**Fig. 8 | Deposition on building surfaces is set by form, but does not overturn the ground-deposition trend.**
**a**, Ratio of wall-and-roof to ground deposition against λp, for three capture distances d_w. **b**,
Height distribution of wall and roof deposits (d_w = 2 m); the dotted line is the mean building height —
59–65 % of the deposits are on roofs. **c**, Rank correlation between deposited fraction and λp with
ground deposition only and with building surfaces included; the value is unchanged across capture distances.
